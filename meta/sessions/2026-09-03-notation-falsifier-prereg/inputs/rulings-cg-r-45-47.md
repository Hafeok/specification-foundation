## CG-R-47 — The three independence residuals are aggregated, not carried separately

The weakest point is correct and irreducible. A brief that carefully tells its reader what not to do is a brief written by someone who knows what the list is for, and no word-level scrub reaches the shape of a document.

**Do not make the brief less careful.** A brief stripped of its prohibitions to seem casual would produce a worse derivation, which is the over-scrubbing rule applied to tone rather than terms.

**Ruled: the three residuals are aggregated into one stated limit**, not filed as three notes:

1. **CG-R-39** — the brief is this session's phrasing of a grid that is itself under test.
2. **CG-R-43** — the receiving session can infer that a falsification programme exists and that a pre-registration tests a closure claim.
3. **This one** — the care in the document's prohibitions discloses that its output matters to something.

Each is individually small and dismissible. Aggregated they describe a single channel: **the receiving session can tell it is doing careful work for a programme that will scrutinise the result, without knowing what the scrutiny is.** That is one limit on independence, and it belongs in the reconciliation as one input to the provenance grade rather than as three footnotes that get weighed and waved through separately.

**What it does not disclose**, and this is why the aggregate is still tolerable: the audit found no frame vocabulary, so the inference does not reach *the list will be counted against something*. Between "this matters" and "this is a denominator" is the whole distance the scrub was for.
