# Invocation — command-slice category derivation

**Paste the text below the line, with the bundle attached. Nothing else.**

Read the pre-flight note first. Do not paste any part of it.

---

Derive the consequential-property categories for a **command slice**.

Attached is a bundle containing the brief, the constructs the categories are drawn from, and a manifest with hashes. Read the brief first; it is the instruction set and it carries the derivation grid, the admission conditions, the naming rules and the output form. Where anything below conflicts with the brief, the brief wins and you say so.

**Work from the bundle only.** Do not clone or read any repository, and do not search the web. Everything the derivation needs is in the bundle; anything not in it is out of scope by design.

**What to produce.** One file: the category list, in the output form the brief specifies, with each row citing the grid cell it came from. Alongside it, a rejection register — every candidate the grid produced that you did not admit, with the admission condition it failed. The rejection register is not optional and is not a summary: it is how the pruning gets checked rather than trusted.

**Standing rules.**

- You propose. You do not ratify your own output, and nothing you produce is settled by your having produced it.
- Keep three registers distinct in your output: what is settled, what is `[PROPOSED]`, what is `[OPEN]`.
- British spelling.
- Errors are corrected by an appended note, never by amendment. A wrong figure stays visible with its correction beside it.
- Report defects honestly, including in the brief and in your own earlier reasoning.
- Name the weakest point of what you produce, rather than leaving it for a reader to find.
- Prose describes what you have actually done. Anything you considered and did not do is marked as such.

**Two rules on the derivation itself.**

Apply the grid exhaustively before pruning. Produce every candidate the grid yields, then admit or reject each against the conditions. A candidate never generated cannot be rejected on the record, and the register is the only evidence the pruning was disciplined.

Each row must stand as a decision point for a command slice **about anything** — any domain, any system. A row that only makes sense for a particular kind of command is out of scope for this list, and saying so is a finding rather than a failure.

**Do not ask what this is for.** The brief contains what you need. If the brief is insufficient, say precisely what is missing and stop; do not fill the gap by inference about intent.

Produce the file, state your weakest point, and stop.
